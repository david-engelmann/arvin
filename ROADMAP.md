## Decision models

Judgment enters arvin between the stages, where the signals conflict.

The deterministic triggers own the edges of the system. Spend rate ceilings, error thresholds, the hard budget ceiling, and the final kill stage fire by ordinary code, without any model involvement, and no model output can veto them. A system that asks a model's permission before its kill switch fires has no kill switch. The first move into warn is likewise deterministic, because the cost of a needless warning is low and the cost of a late one is not.

The middle is genuinely ambiguous, and that is where the model works. Spend rising while errors stay flat, or a loop detector and a progress signal disagreeing, are states where escalation and patience are both defensible and the right call depends on the shape of the whole picture. For those states the model returns a score for runaway likelihood and a choice among hold and escalate and de-escalate for the middle stages, warn and throttle and cut tools, with a calibrated confidence and per label probabilities.

The combination rule is asymmetric on purpose, because the two error directions cost different things. An escalation the model recommends is applied at a modest confidence bar, since a needless throttle is recoverable and a missed runaway is not. A de-escalation requires a high confidence bar and, in addition, the deterministic signals that raised the stage must actually have cleared, checked by ordinary code. Abstention or a failed call holds the current stage. The model can therefore make arvin faster to protect itself and can never make it slower, and the downward path stays guarded by evidence a person can recheck without trusting the model at all.

The model is Typesafe Jev, reached only through the program's Typesafe connector route, a System One decision model returning typed choices and scores. The OpenAI decision model is the named comparison point. The same recorded telemetry vignettes are scored by both models in evaluation, and wiring the comparison model waits on a route and a budget set at program level. Every stage decision the model influenced is recorded with the model name, the score, the verdict, the confidence, and the deterministic signal state at the time, because a kill switch whose history cannot be reconstructed will not be trusted the first time it fires on a system its operator loves.

What stays deterministic is everything with an irreversible or expensive consequence. The stage machinery, the triggers, the ceilings, the kill, the signal checks behind any de-escalation, and the record of what happened are ordinary code. The model reads a picture and renders a judgment inside that envelope. It holds no switch.

Tests stay offline. A bounded recording window captures synthetic telemetry vignettes and the model's responses and stores them in the repo as JSON fixtures, each fixture carrying the model name and the exact call shape. Every vignette is synthetic, invented for this repo, because nothing real ever enters this repo. The suite replays the fixtures through a fixture transport and no test performs a network call. The asymmetric combination rule is tested purely deterministically at its edges, a confident de-escalation against signals that have not cleared, an abstention at every stage, and an escalation recommendation arriving the same moment a deterministic ceiling fires, so the safety properties are pinned by tests that owe nothing to any model's mood.

The live call budget for this repo is a call count budget, set by the repo owner under the program standing delegation of 2026-11-02, because a decision call is small. The budget is at most 10 verification calls while the client shim is built, at most 200 calls in the single fixture recording window, and at most 100 live evaluation calls in the round that lands a decision feature, with the recorded usage printed in that round's gate report. At the vendor's claimed price the expected spend across all three allowances is under a cent, and the binding cap is the count, with a hard stop of 5 dollars per repo per month if real pricing or call sizes differ from the claim. Any surprise is reported before any further call is made.
